# Moderation baselines for a public UGC game

**Research date:** 2026-09-08  
**Status:** Planning research for the One Fact Changed adults-only public beta; **not legal advice**.

## Scope and decision

This baseline covers a browser-based service with open registration and public user-generated: (1) premises, (2) responses consisting of one final claim and three causal steps, (3) nickname profiles, (4) votes, and (5) comments. The beta has no direct messages, user media, or commercial/outbound promotion. AI screens submitted premises; this baseline does not assume AI moderation on the other surfaces.

**Recommended beta baseline:** keep the beta text-only and public; pre-screen every premise before publication; apply deterministic input controls plus user reporting and human review to responses, nickname profiles, and comments; treat vote anomalies only as review signals; publish one short, surface-specific content policy; and provide notice and a human appeal for every content or account restriction except where notice would compromise a legally directed investigation or expose protected material. A model score must never be the sole basis for a permanent account ban, a legal conclusion, or denial of an appeal. OpenAI itself describes moderation scores as policy signals rather than automatic blocking decisions, and warns that score-based policies may need recalibration as models change.[14]

“Adults only” is a product rule, not proof that minors cannot enter. Ofcom says an over-18 term is not highly effective age assurance, and the European Commission's non-binding 2025 guidance similarly says an adult-only declaration without effective age-assurance measures does not establish that minors cannot access a platform.[10][7]

The two classes below must not be conflated:

- **A — Conditional legal or contractual launch gates:** mandatory only when the stated jurisdiction, provider type, service model, size, knowledge, or vendor trigger applies. The launch owner must record whether each trigger applies before opening that market.
- **B — Community-policy choices:** the recommended house rules for this beta. They are not represented as universal legal requirements, although particular content may also activate a Gate A duty.

## B. Recommended beta community policy

These are optional house-policy choices unless a Gate A trigger independently makes an action mandatory.
These categories consolidate Ofcom's current priority-illegal-content taxonomy with a mature platform's current rules for user safety, privacy, authenticity, spam, and rank abuse.[19][20]

### Publish these content boundaries

**Prohibit on every surface:**

1. child sexual exploitation or sexualization, grooming, or sexual solicitation involving a minor;
2. credible threats, praise/recruitment for violent extremist activity, or actionable instructions to seriously harm a person;
3. targeted harassment, stalking, hateful dehumanization, or repeated unwanted targeting;
4. doxxing or sharing credentials, private contact/location information, intimate information, or other personal data without permission;
5. non-consensual sexual content, sexual extortion, sexual services, and graphic sexual content;
6. encouragement or instructions for suicide, self-injury, or disordered eating (support-seeking and recovery discussion remain allowed);
7. fraud, phishing, malware, illegal transactions, or instructions whose practical purpose is evasion or abuse;
8. impersonation intended to deceive, spam, advertising, commercial solicitation, or outbound links;
9. bots, coordinated or multi-account vote manipulation, or trading value for votes; and
10. material that infringes another person's intellectual property.

**Allow when the context does not cross those boundaries:** disagreement; criticism; non-targeted profanity; fiction and counterfactual premises; reporting, educational, historical, recovery, or condemnatory discussion of harmful subjects; and quoted or reclaimed identity terms. Context matters: conversational-context research found that surrounding messages can be vital even for human abusive-language annotation and that context remains difficult for classifiers.[15]

### Apply the policy by surface

| Surface | Beta control and consequence |
|---|---|
| **Submitted premise** | AI screens the complete premise before publication and holds any suspected policy violation for a trained human. Deterministic form violations, such as an outbound link or exceeded field limit, may be rejected automatically with the applicable form rule. The reviewer may publish, reject with a rule citation and resubmission, or start the Gate A escalation. |
| **Response: final claim** | Evaluate the final claim together with its premise and all three steps. A safety score is not a truth, causation, or defamation check. Reject the whole response rather than silently rewriting the user's claim. |
| **Response: three causal steps** | Evaluate each step and the chain as a whole: individually innocuous steps can compose into operational harmful instructions. If one step violates policy, reject the response with the relevant rule and permit a clean resubmission. |
| **Nickname profile** | Reject or force-rename explicit slurs used as attacks, child-sexual references, deceptive impersonation, promotions/links, and exposed personal contact information. Use a neutral temporary name while a rename is reviewed; do not infer identity or intent from an identity term alone. |
| **Comments** | Provide per-comment reporting and reviewer access to the premise, response, parent comment, and nearby thread. Remove targeted abuse or private data; use a warning or temporary comment restriction for repeated lower-severity abuse; lock a thread only when narrower action does not stop the harm. |
| **Votes** | State only the policy boundary: one person may not automate, buy, coordinate, or multiply accounts to manipulate votes. Velocity, account age, and clusters are leads, not proof. Quarantine suspect influence and obtain human review before an account penalty; do not publish detection mechanics. |

## Severity × confidence enforcement

“Confidence” means confidence that the content matches the published rule, not confidence that it is illegal. Model category scores are only one signal.[14]

| Severity | High confidence | Medium confidence | Low or context-dependent confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S4 — Critical safety/legal:** apparent child exploitation; credible imminent threat; sexual extortion; exposed credentials or precise live location | Prevent publication/remove access; temporarily secure the account; preserve only required evidence; page the trained human/legal runbook immediately. | Hold privately and obtain immediate trained-human review; send a neutral hold notice unless a specialist runbook permits a delay or narrower notice to protect a victim, evidence, or investigation. | Keep non-public only if the potential harm is time-critical; otherwise priority-review without account penalty. |
| **S3 — Serious targeted harm:** doxxing; targeted violent harassment; harmful operational instructions; fraud/phishing | Remove or hold; temporary account restriction; priority human review. Escalate under Gate A only if its actual trigger is met. | Hold and review before publication where possible; no permanent penalty without a human. | Allow only if no imminent exposure is created; queue with full context and rely on user reporting. |
| **S2 — Ordinary policy abuse:** non-credible harassment/hate; spam/promotion; repeated disruption; clear impersonation | Reject/remove with warning; apply a short surface restriction for repetition. | Add friction or queue; do not penalize the account before review. | Allow, log a sample for quality review, and keep reporting available. |
| **S1 — Borderline/allowed context:** quotation, counterspeech, fiction, profanity, identity discussion, support-seeking | Usually allow; if the narrow rule is genuinely met, use the least restrictive content action. | Allow. | Allow. |

A single severe event or suspected active compromise can justify an immediate temporary restriction; permanent account removal always requires a documented human decision. Repetition should increase consequence only when prior decisions are final and materially similar. Do not count reversed decisions as strikes.

## Operational controls required for this baseline

1. **Submission controls.** Enforce short field limits, reject URLs and promotion, preserve the raw text for review, and create a normalized view for spacing/Unicode confusables without replacing the evidentiary original. Rate-limit registration, posting, reporting, and voting. Publish the staffed UGC language; hold any submission the queue cannot competently assess.
2. **Context-aware review.** Send the premise, final claim, and all three causal steps to response review; send the parent and nearby comments to comment review. The model-independent reason is practical: isolated-message moderation loses information, while context-aware detection is itself imperfect.[15]
3. **Human queue.** Separate S4 from ordinary work; limit S4 access to trained reviewers; provide a second reviewer for permanent bans, denied high-impact appeals, and ambiguous identity/context cases. The Santa Clara Principles recommend understandable rules, cultural competence, notice, appeals, accuracy monitoring, and high confidence before automated removals; they are voluntary principles, not law.[12]
4. **Fail safe by surface.** If premise screening fails, keep the premise pending rather than publishing it. If optional automation for another surface fails, keep ordinary public content reportable and rate-limited; do not silently ban it. An unavailable classifier returns no safety conclusion.
5. **Decision record.** For each action store a case ID, surface/item ID, relevant snapshot or hash, reported rule, severity, signals and model/version, reviewer and reason, timestamps, scope/duration, notices, appeal, reversal, and any legally required hold. Restrict sensitive evidence and apply a documented retention/deletion schedule; the special one-year §2258A preservation rule applies only when that statute's report trigger is met.[1]
6. **Quality control.** Before launch and after any model/version, threshold, policy, locale, or UI-context change, run a fixed set containing allowed counterspeech/fiction/support and prohibited threats/doxxing/evasion. Sample both allowed and blocked premises; measure false positives, misses found by reports, reversal rate, decision time, and results by supported language. Research demonstrates that small natural-looking perturbations can transfer-attack multiple toxicity systems, group mentions can produce disproportionate text-classifier false positives, and identical hate-speech text can receive markedly different results across systems.[16][17][18]
7. **Operational targets (house targets, not statutory deadlines).** Route credible imminent threats and apparent child exploitation immediately; make an initial S3 decision within 24 hours, other reports within 72 hours, and appeals within seven calendar days. If capacity cannot meet the S4 path, pause new submissions rather than accumulate an unseen critical queue.
8. **Transparency.** Publish the policy, action types, use of AI on premises, reporting/appeal route, and aggregated counts for reports, removals, account restrictions, automation-originated cases, appeals, and reversals. Never publish information that identifies reporters or victims.

## Concrete automation limits and fallback by surface
Published vendor classifiers cover only named taxonomies. For example, OpenAI's current moderation categories do not expose named classifications for private information or doxxing, impersonation, spam or promotion, copyright, or vote manipulation.[14] Every candidate model needs a coverage map; an absent category is not a safe score.

| Surface | What automation cannot safely decide | Required beta fallback |
|---|---|---|
| **Premises** | A standalone classifier can confuse fictional, quoted, condemnatory, or safety-aware discussion with endorsement; even vendor documentation notes that a refusal discussing harm may be flagged.[14] Euphemisms, spacing, and other natural-looking perturbations can evade toxicity systems.[16] | Use AI for triage and pre-publication holding only. Give a reviewer the entire premise and policy; calibrate per language/category/model; never equate a score with illegality. |
| **Final claims** | A toxicity model cannot establish factual truth, causation, defamation, or the target's identity. Different moderation systems draw materially different hate-speech boundaries for identical text.[18] | Do not add automated truth enforcement. Use reports and a human reading the premise, claim, and steps; obtain specialist/legal review for a concrete defamation or threat issue. |
| **Causal steps** | Step-by-step harm can emerge from the combination even when each short step appears benign; isolated classification also loses the premise and final-claim context.[15] | Review the full chain. Use deterministic checks only as routing signals, and reject/resubmit rather than machine-editing one step. |
| **Nickname profiles** | Very short strings provide little context; identity-group mentions can attract disproportionate false positives in text toxicity detection, especially in ambiguous samples.[17] Unicode lookalikes and reclaimed terms also defeat simple blocklists or create overblocking. | Use narrow deterministic rules for URLs/contact details and unmistakable prohibited strings; route ambiguous identity/impersonation cases to a human and offer rename appeal. |
| **Comments** | Sarcasm, quotation, counterspeech, reply targets, dialect, and code-switching depend on conversational and cultural context.[12][15] | Keep parent/thread context in the report; use user reports and human review. Any optional classifier only prioritizes the queue. |
| **Votes** | A text model has no relevant evidence, and anomaly signals cannot distinguish legitimate consensus, shared networks, publicity, or coordination by themselves. | Use privacy-reviewed telemetry as a lead; quarantine impact if necessary, then require human review before invalidation or account action. Keep the policy boundary public and mechanics private. |
| **Cross-surface/account** | Aggregating weak model flags can amplify the same bias, and switching models can change outcomes.[17][18] | Do not sum raw scores into automatic strikes. Show reviewers the underlying items and final/reversed history; require a human for lasting account action. |

The initial beta should therefore **not** expand AI enforcement beyond premise triage. Add automated routing on another surface only after a surface- and language-specific evaluation shows a useful error trade-off and the human fallback is staffed.

## Notice and appeal flow

1. **Report:** every premise, response, nickname profile, and comment has an accessible report action; the vote/account route covers suspected manipulation. Ask for the item, policy reason, optional context, and optional reporter contact. Where DSA Article 16 applies, provide a no-gameplay-account legal-notice route available to any individual or entity. The form must facilitate notices containing the exact electronic location, a reasoned explanation of alleged illegality, the notifier's name and email except for notices concerning offences in Articles 3–7 of Directive 2011/93/EU, and a good-faith accuracy/completeness statement; acknowledge receipt and, in the decision notice, disclose any use of automated means.[5]
2. **Triage:** assign case ID, severity, potential Gate A trigger, and reviewer queue. Preserve ordinary evidence only under the retention policy; a legal hold or special statutory preservation rule overrides deletion only within its scope.
3. **Decision:** select no action, reject/restore, reduce visibility, remove, rename, invalidate votes, warn, or temporarily/permanently restrict. Record one policy clause and item-specific facts.
4. **Notice:** tell the affected user what item/account was acted on, action and duration, facts relied on, policy and—when applicable—legal basis, whether automation contributed, and how and by when to appeal. DSA Article 17 requires clear, specific reasons for covered restrictions; UK section 21 requires an easy and transparent complaints procedure for covered complaints.[5][9]
5. **Appeal:** accept the case ID, disputed conclusion, context/evidence, and requested remedy for at least 30 days as the house baseline (or longer where Gate A requires, including DSA Article 20's six months for a covered non-exempt platform).[5] Do not require the user to reproduce prohibited or protected material.
6. **Review:** a human not bound by the initial score rechecks the original policy version, full context, proportionality, and new evidence. A different human must review a proposed denial when the first reviewer made the original permanent-ban/high-severity decision. GitHub's first-party process is a useful operational precedent: appeals are human-decided, a different reviewer independently checks an initial reviewer's proposed denial, and mistakes or excessive severity can be reversed or modified.[13]
7. **Outcome:** send a reasoned result; restore content/account/votes promptly when reversed, remove the strike, and correct downstream visibility. Give any applicable statutory or out-of-court redress route. Aggregate the result into quality metrics.

For apparent child exploitation, credible threats, legally restricted evidence, or active law-enforcement process, the specialist runbook determines what notice can safely and lawfully be sent. An ordinary appeal never displays victim material or defeats a preservation obligation. A DMCA counter-notice follows its separate statutory process.[1][3]

## Minimum launch checklist

### Gate A — must be complete for every enabled market

- [ ] Launch geographies, targeting, legal entity, user counts, provider/service classification, and micro/small/VLOP status are recorded with a dated owner decision.
- [ ] U.S. §2258A applicability and NCMEC escalation/preservation access are counsel-reviewed; COPPA data map, neutral age screen, under-13 actual-knowledge path, privacy notice, vendor disclosures, and retention are approved.[1][2]
- [ ] If EU in scope: DSA contacts/legal representative, clear terms, Article 16 notice form, Article 17 reasons, Article 18 escalation, Article 15 reporting or a recorded Article 15(2) exception, size-exemption analysis, complaint/redress path, and minors-access analysis are live.[5][7][8]
- [ ] If UK in scope: sections 3–4 classification; sections 9/10 assessment and controls; sections 20/21 reports and complaints; section 23 records; sections 35–37 children's access assessment; and section 66 CSEA route are complete.[9][10][11]
- [ ] If seeking U.S. §512(c) safe harbor: registered/current public agent, notice/counter-notice, and repeat-infringer process are live.[3][4]
- [ ] Moderation, hosting, analytics, age, and support vendors are selected; signed terms cover input authorization, data use/retention/deletion, security, locations/subprocessors, model changes, outages, exports, and incident/legal-report responsibility. Until selection, vendor compliance is unresolved and launch remains gated.

### Gate B — recommended product baseline

- [ ] The ten content boundaries and allowed-context examples are published for all five surfaces.
- [ ] Premises fail closed into a staffed human queue; no other surface has final AI enforcement; all surfaces have rate limits and reporting.
- [ ] S4 and S3 runbooks, reviewer access, coverage, escalation contacts, and capacity targets have been exercised with realistic scenarios.
- [ ] Notices name the item, action, rule, reason, duration, automation, and appeal path; appeals are human, can reverse every downstream consequence, and use an independent second reviewer for high-impact denials.
- [ ] Audit records, sensitive-evidence access, legal holds, ordinary retention/deletion, quality sampling, model/version tracking, rollback, and aggregate metrics are operational.
- [ ] Adversarial, contextual, identity-term, multilingual, false-positive, and false-negative premise cases have been evaluated against the exact production workflow after every material model or policy change.[14][15][16][17][18]
- [ ] No direct messages, user media, commercial promotion, or outbound links are enabled; adding any is a fresh safety and Gate A assessment, not an assumption carried from this text-only beta.

**Separate follow-up ticket:** none recommended from this research. The unresolved launch-geography, service/provider/size, children-access, and vendor facts are explicit launch gates above; existing product tickets own the detailed policy authority and nickname/account decisions.
